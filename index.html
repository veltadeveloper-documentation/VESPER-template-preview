04 — Notes from the studio

The Notes.

Longer thinking on colour, regulation, and 6 a.m. batches. No tips, no listicles — the letters we'd write anyway.

Soho didn't start as a red. It started as a complaint — a customer in the shop telling us that every “bright” shade she owned read pink on her, and every “true red” read orange. We made her a red with a drop more blue in the base, and she wore it out of the shop before we'd labelled it.

Every shade since has gone through the same door: someone real, a specific complaint, a formula rebuilt until the complaint disappears. Nerve, we've found, is half the product.

Here's the uncomfortable bit: in the UK and the EU, “clean” isn't a regulated term. Anyone can print it. What is regulated — heavily — is the safety assessment behind every formula, the CPSR, which every product we make files before it's ever filled.

So when a brand leans on the word “clean” and not on its documentation, squint. Our full CPSR summaries ship with every order — dull reading, but it's the only kind that counts.

The fill line runs before the bakery next door does. Bullets get poured at temperature, cooled for forty minutes, then weighed — every single one — on the same brass scale. Underweight bullets go back into the pot; there's no “close enough” pile.

By noon the day's batch is boxed, and the tins are stamped by hand with the date they were made. If yours says a Tuesday, it was a Tuesday.
